### About 
NetSci is the flagship conference of the Network Science Society and the premier venue for sharing and discussing discoveries, ideas, and emerging challenges in network science. Its program spans the many ways networks shape our world: social and information networks, epidemics and public health, ecological and biological systems, the brain, infrastructure, and AI-driven systems.

NetSci 2027 is organised by the Center Synergy of Systems (SynoSys) at TU Dresden and takes place in Dresden, Germany, from 17 to 21 May 2027: satellites and the school on 17–18 May, followed by the main conference from 18 to 21 May.

Network science has always grown by crossing borders: between physics and biology, between computation and the social sciences, between theory and the messy data of the real world. NetSci 2027 is about breaking the field's current boundaries. Which questions are we not yet asking? Which systems, data, and communities have we not yet reached?

We invite work that is forward-looking and willing to take risks, that connects network science to new problems and disciplines, and that challenges how we think about networks. And we want young researchers at the centre of it: the people who will define where the field goes next.

### Call for Abstracts
Submit your work on any topic in network science as a one-page abstract. Deadline: 1 December 2026.

We invite one-page abstracts across all areas of network science, and especially work that breaks the field's current boundaries. Each abstract includes one mandatory descriptive figure (with caption) and references. Abstracts are reviewed by the Program Committee and accepted as a contributed talk or a poster. Authors may indicate a preferred format; the Program Committee makes the final decision.

Abstract requirements:
- Abstracts must be no longer than one page, including text, figure, and references.
- Submissions must be in PDF format, prepared with the official LaTeX template.
- The language of submission and presentation is English.
- Each accepted abstract must be presented in person by one of its authors.
- Each author may present only one abstract at the main conference.
- Presenting authors must register by the presenter deadline to appear in the final program.
- Use of generative AI: Authors are fully responsible for the accuracy, originality, and scholarly quality of their abstracts; generative AI may assist with language and editing, but not replace the authors' own scientific contribution. Irresponsible use of generative AI for fabricating content (such as invented results, figures, or references) will not be accepted: such submissions will be desk rejected, and repeated occurrence will be recorded.
