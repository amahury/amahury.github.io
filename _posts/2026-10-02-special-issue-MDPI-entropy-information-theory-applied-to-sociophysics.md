### Special Issue Information
Dear Colleagues,

Sociophysics applies concepts from statistical physics and information theory to understand collective social phenomena emerging from interactions among many agents. Processes such as opinion formation, information spreading, polarization, and consensus can be modeled as stochastic dynamical systems whose macroscopic behavior arises from microscopic interaction rules.

Entropy plays a central role in this framework, providing quantitative measures of uncertainty, diversity, and order in social systems. From an information-theoretic perspective, social dynamics can be interpreted as the evolution of probability distributions over collective states, often exhibiting phase transitions, metastability, and relaxation toward stationary configurations.

This Special Issue aims to compile contributions that use entropy-based and statistical–mechanical approaches to model, analyze, and interpret social and socio-economic systems. We welcome theoretical, computational, and data-driven studies that advance the understanding of collective behavior from a sociophysics perspective.

Topics of interest include (but are not limited to) the following:
- Entropy and information-theoretic measures in sociophysics models;
- Opinion dynamics, consensus, polarization, and fragmentation;
- Information spreading, rumor dynamics, and social contagion;
- Socio-economic systems modeled as interacting particle systems;
- Phase transitions and critical phenomena in social dynamics;
- Stochastic and agent-based models of collective behavior;
- Network structure, heterogeneity, and their role in social entropy;
- The empirical validation of sociophysics models using social data.
