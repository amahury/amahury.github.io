Fellows join a multidisciplinary cohort of five and work on a project of their own, embedded in the Lab’s day-to-day research life.

The Lab’s work is anchored in philosophy but explicitly multidisciplinary: we welcome applicants from law, political science, economics, psychology, computer science and adjacent fields.

### Research programmes
Every application must indicate one of the following.
- **Theories of Victory and Viable Endgames**. Positive futures: what a good outcome from advanced AI concretely looks like, and what would have to be true for us to reach it.
- **Democratic Control over AI Companies**. AI-enabled power concentration in frontier AI companies, and the institutional, legal and political mechanisms through which democratic societies could exercise meaningful control over them.
- **Other**. Strong proposals outside these two programmes will be considered, provided the contribution to AI safety is clear.

### What you get
- Three months in residence at the Lab’s offices in Leuven, with a desk and access to KU Leuven’s libraries and facilities.
- A monthly allowance of €2,000, covering accommodation and other living costs.
- Formal status as a visiting scholar at KU Leuven — no employment contract; fellows remain responsible for their own tax and social-security situation.
- Supervision and feedback from the Lab’s core team, and the opportunity to present work in progress at internal seminars.
- The opportunity to present at the Lab’s annual conference and summer school.

### What we ask
- Presence. Fellows are expected at the Leuven office regularly during the fellowship. We do not prescribe a number of days: the programme is built around a shared office, where a question can be put to a colleague on the spot rather than saved for a scheduled call. It suits researchers who want that, and remote participation is not possible.
- Output. A completed paper by the end of the fellowship, deposited as a preprint on arXiv, PhilPapers or an equivalent repository, and submitted to a target venue identified in the research plan.
- Dissemination. Fellows present their work at the Lab and actively disseminate it afterwards.
- Contribution to the Lab’s activities. Fellows assist with the annual conference and/or the summer school — both of which also give them a platform for their own work.
- Co-authorship. While not a requirement, we encourage co-authored papers.

### Eligibility
PhD candidates, postdoctoral researchers, faculty, and researchers based outside academia. Applicants from outside academia should make clear that the stay serves a research purpose. We particularly encourage applications from women, minorities and underrepresented groups.

### Submission
- Curriculum vitae. Max. 2 pages, including a list of publications.
- Cover letter. Max. 1 page: motivation, fit with the Lab, and what you hope to gain from the stay.
- Research plan. Max. 1,500 words, covering the research question and why it matters for AI safety; the methodology; a theory of change (what you expect the work to affect, and through what route); the journals, conferences and workshops you intend to submit to; why the project is feasible within three months; and the research programme you are applying for.

### How we choose
We assess applications on:
- Quality and originality of the proposed research.
- Fit with one of the research programmes.
- The plausibility of the theory of change — academic, policy or field-building impact.
- Feasibility within three months.
- What the applicant would contribute to the Lab’s research community.

### Key dates
- 21 September 2026 | Call published
- 31 October 2026 | Application deadline
- November 2026 | Interviews (shortlist)
- End of November 2026 | Successful applicants contacted
- March – May 2027 | First cohort in residence

### Contact 
Send your application to existentialrisk@kuleuven.be, with “Fellowship application — [programme]” in the subject line. Questions about the programme can be sent to the same address.
