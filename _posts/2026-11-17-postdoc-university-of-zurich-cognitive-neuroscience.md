A four-year postdoctoral position is available at the Taylor Lab at the University of Zürich Psychology Department, full-time and fully-funded.

Our research group investigates how perception and action interact – especially how we see when we move through the world, or when the world moves past us. This position is part of the SNSF-funded project “Predictive Processing in Dynamic Scenes: decoding the top down modulation of optic flow”. The main responsibility is to lead research projects testing psychological models of predictive coding and attention using dynamic, naturalistic scenes. Methods include EEG and mobile EEG, visual flicker, machine learning, psychophysics and transcranial magnetic stimulation.

Applicants should have or be about to finish a PhD in a relevant discipline. Anticipated start date is between January and April 2027, negotiable.

You do not need to have used all of those particular methods before but you should have experience at testing cognitive theories using neuroscientific techniques. You have excellent spoken and written English, and are motivated to push the boundaries of what we know about the neural basis of attention and prediction in real world dynamic contexts, and can work in a team.

Please E-Mail Paul Taylor a single PDF by December 1st 2026 including your CV, a short letter highlighting motivation and experience, and email addresses of one or two referees.

Informal enquiries are welcome. Further information about our research is available on the Taylor Lab website.

We also work with graduate students and colleagues interested in human visual cognition, neural dynamics and the development of experimental methods.

Source and more details: [https://paultaylorbrain.github.io/join.html](https://paultaylorbrain.github.io/join.html)
