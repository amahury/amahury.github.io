### About 
The Allen Institute is a leader in performing large-scale, standardized biological science, from cellular structure to the human immune system, working since 2003 to generate foundational knowledge and tools. Biology’s toughest questions require not only new ways to observe the complex systems of life, but new mathematical, computational, and theoretical approaches to living systems. The next stage of discovery depends on novel connections, novel tools, and a novel way of approaching science.

Studio D3 (data-driven discovery) was created to attract a new generation of scientists from diverse quantitative and mathematical backgrounds, both those experienced in biology and those new to the field, bringing fresh perspectives to some of the field’s most complex challenges. As a Fellow, you’ll step into a highly collaborative, studio-style environment where data, theory, and cutting-edge computation converge—working alongside Institute scientists and a global network of Affiliates to turn complex datasets into new biological insight. Reaching across various biological domains, with the freedom to pursue creative, high-impact ideas and the support of a deeply integrated scientific community, Studio D3 Fellows are empowered to push boundaries, integrate bold ideas, and redefine how discovery happens.

### Applications 
Applications for Fall 2027 will open September 15th and close December 1st, 2026.

Selected candidates will be invited to give virtual talks on February 8th and 9th, 2027. A subset of those candidates will then be invited for in-person interviews on March 8th and 9th.

Fellowship details:
- 3-year program for recent Ph.D.s to explore complex biological questions at the Allen Institute to drive discovery.
- Designed to provide freedom to explore novel computational research areas of interest to enhance the mission Allen Institute’s accelerators.
- ‍Mentorship from leading Allen Institute scientists and Affiliates. Fellows are hired as an Allen Institute employee with a starting salary of $111,100 annually, immigration support and benefits package.
- ‍Biology experience helpful but not necessary—we're seeking applicants with expertise in computer science, statistics, engineering, mathematics, physics, and other quantitative fields, both those experienced in biology as well as those new to the study of living systems.

You do not need experience in biology to apply. Applicants must be scientists with a Ph.D. (or equivalent) or who will have completed their Ph.D. by the start of the fellowship. The Ph.D. should have been awarded in the last 3 years. The fellowship is appropriate for Ph.D.s in a quantitative field such as computer science, electrical engineering, physics, mathematics, or statistics. Up to three fellows will be selected each year. International applicants are eligible to apply and we provide immigration support.

Application guidelines:
- A curriculum vitae (CV) from the applicant.
- Two (2) Letters of support from your mentor and other professors and scientists with whom you have worked.
- A one-page personal statement from the applicant describing their history, activities, and interests as an individual (especially those outside of science) which may give the committee a greater sense of you as a person and your ability to simultaneously interact with and challenge traditional scientific fields.
- A two page research statement from the applicant describing their prior research, potential research interests (including tools they are familiar with), and how this fellowship aligns with their career goals.
- A one page mock research proposal on a biological question that interests you and how you would approach it with your skill set. You will not be held to this proposal should you be accepted nor (for those new to biology) will you be judged on biological knowledge you are not yet expected to have.

Application and more details: [https://alleninstitute.org/studio-d3/fellowship](https://alleninstitute.org/studio-d3/fellowship)
